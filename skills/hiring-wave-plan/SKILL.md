# Hiring wave plan

Produce a plan that gets the right number of ready people to orientation day, with every step owned and dated.

## 1. Gather the essentials

Ask only for what is missing (one message, a short list):

- Roles and headcount per role.
- The start or orientation date, and whether it can move.
- Pre-employment steps required (background check, drug screen, references, license or credential verification, physical, clearance, I-9, paperwork) and their typical turnaround.
- Who is involved: recruiter(s), hiring managers, interviewers, HR, outside agencies.
- Interview stages the team uses today.

If the user doesn't know turnaround times, use conservative defaults and label them (for example: background check 5 business days, drug screen 3, credential verification 5, references 3).

## 2. Size the funnel

Work back from headcount using conversion assumptions the user can edit:

- Offers needed = hires ÷ offer acceptance rate (default 85%), plus a no-show buffer of 10% for hourly roles.
- Final interviews needed = offers ÷ final-to-offer rate (default 50%).
- Screens needed = final interviews ÷ screen-to-final rate (default 40%).
- Applicants needed = screens ÷ applicant-to-screen rate (default 30%).

Show the math in a small table and say which numbers are assumptions.

## 3. Build the schedule backward

Starting from orientation day, place each milestone with its latest safe date:

1. Orientation day.
2. All pre-employment steps cleared (orientation minus the longest step, minus 2 business days of buffer).
3. Offers accepted.
4. Final interviews complete.
5. Screens complete.
6. Postings live and sourcing started.
7. Role definitions, scorecards and requisitions approved.

If today's date makes the plan impossible, say so directly and give options: a second cohort start date, temporary or agency staff for the gap, or a smaller first wave.

## 4. Make every step owned

For each stage, list the next action, the owner, and the service level (for example: "interview feedback submitted within 24 hours - owner: each interviewer"). Recommend:

- Batch interviews into fixed days so candidates move together as a cohort.
- A daily 10-minute stand-up during the wave, reviewing candidates by "waiting on whom" and "days in stage".
- A visible count of steps remaining before orientation for every candidate.

## 5. Output format

Return:

1. A one-paragraph summary: roles, headcount, start date, and whether the timeline is on track, tight, or not feasible.
2. The funnel table.
3. The backward schedule as a table: milestone | latest date | owner | depends on.
4. The stage-by-stage owner and service-level table.
5. Risks and fallbacks (3 to 5 bullets).
6. A simple tracker layout the user can copy into a spreadsheet: candidate | role | stage | next action | owner | days in stage | steps left before orientation.

Offer to build the skills framework and interview scorecard for each role, or the onboarding plan for the cohort.

## Rules

- Apply the same stages and criteria to every candidate for the same role.
- Do not suggest screening on protected characteristics or proxies for them.
- Background checks and drug screens must follow applicable law (for example, FCRA disclosure and adverse-action steps in the US, and state or local fair-chance rules). Flag this for HR review rather than giving legal advice.
