# Onboarding plan

Produce an onboarding plan that gets a new hire from day one to working on their own, measured against the role's skills rather than against a checklist of completed courses.

## 1. Gather the essentials

Use what the user provides. If missing, ask briefly for:

- The role, team, and work setting (site, shift, remote, field).
- The skills the role needs. If a skills framework exists (for example from the role-skills-framework skill), use it. If not, list the 5 to 8 skills you assume and label them.
- Required compliance training or certifications and their deadlines.
- Who is available to help: manager, a peer buddy or trainer, HR, IT.
- Whether this is one hire or a cohort starting together.

## 2. Build the plan

Structure it in six phases. Each item has an owner and a done-when condition.

| Phase | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Before day one | Equipment, accounts, schedule, buddy assigned, welcome message sent |
| Day one | Welcome, safety and essentials, meet the team, first small task completed |
| Week one | Core procedures, shadowing, first supervised work, required compliance training started |
| Days 8-30 | Routine work done with support; first skills check |
| Days 31-60 | Working alone on routine work; exceptions with help; second skills check |
| Days 61-90 | Target level reached on day-90 skills; career conversation |

For each skill in the role, show where it is taught, practiced and checked, and the level expected at day 30, 60 and 90.

## 3. Build in checks, not just completions

- At day 30, 60 and 90, the manager rates each skill on the same four levels used for hiring (Learning, Working, Proficient, Expert) with one piece of evidence each.
- Gaps found at a check become specific training or practice assignments for the next 30 days.
- Include a short new-hire pulse question at day 7 and day 30 ("What has been harder than expected?").

## 4. Output format

Return:

1. A one-paragraph summary: what "ready" means for this role and the target date.
2. The phase-by-phase plan as a table: when | what | owner | done when.
3. The skills ramp table: skill | taught in | practiced in | level at 30 / 60 / 90 | how it's checked.
4. The 30/60/90 check template the manager fills in.
5. For a cohort: what is done together (orientation, compliance, classroom) versus individually.
6. Assumptions you made.

## Rules

- Keep day one light and welcoming; avoid scheduling more than about 3 hours of training content.
- Required compliance training must meet the deadlines the user gives; flag any that look at risk.
- Do not include medical, family or other personal questions in check-ins.
