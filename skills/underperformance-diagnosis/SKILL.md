# Underperformance diagnosis

Help the user find the real cause of underperformance before they spend money on the wrong fix. Most performance problems are a mix of three causes:

- **Can't** - the skills or knowledge are missing. Fix: hiring criteria, training, practice, coaching.
- **Won't** - the skills are there, but motivation or engagement is not. Fix: management, recognition, workload, career path, fit.
- **Blocked** - people could and would, but something in the system stops them: unclear expectations, broken process, missing tools, staffing, conflicting priorities. Fix: management and process changes, not training.

## 1. Understand the symptom

Ask briefly (one message) for whatever is missing:

- What is underperforming, measured how, compared with what (target, other teams, last year)?
- Who is affected: one person, one team or shift, one site, or a whole role?
- When did it start, and what changed around then (new manager, system, product, policy, rapid hiring, layoffs)?
- What has already been tried?

## 2. Test the three causes

Work through the evidence for each cause. Use comparisons wherever possible, because they separate causes quickly:

- **Same role, different results** across teams, shifts or sites with similar conditions points to management or local process (blocked or won't), not skills.
- **New hires struggling while experienced people are fine** points to hiring criteria or onboarding (can't).
- **People who could do it before and stopped** points to won't or blocked.
- **Everyone struggling after a change** (new system, procedure, product) points to can't (training gap on the change) or blocked (the change itself is flawed).

Check the seven areas below and rate each as Likely, Possible, or Unlikely to be contributing, with the evidence:

1. People - skills and knowledge against what the role needs.
2. Leadership - clarity, feedback and coaching from managers.
3. Work design - process, tools, staffing levels, workload.
4. Accountability - clear goals, measures and consequences.
5. Engagement - motivation, recognition, morale.
6. Talent sustainability - turnover, reliance on a few key people, succession.
7. Business alignment - whether goals and priorities conflict across teams.

## 3. Recommend how to confirm

Give the 3 to 5 fastest ways to confirm the diagnosis, for example:

- A short skills check of the affected team against the role's requirements (does the team actually have the skills?).
- Comparing the affected team with a well-performing team in the same role.
- Five skip-level conversations using a few neutral questions.
- Walking the process with the people who do the work and timing where it stalls.

## 4. Output format

Return:

1. The likely cause mix in one sentence (for example: "Mostly blocked, partly can't").
2. A table of the seven areas: area | likely / possible / unlikely | evidence | what would confirm it.
3. What to do first and what not to do yet (for example: "Do not roll out training to everyone until the skills check shows a gap").
4. The confirmation steps with owners and a two-week timeline.
5. A priority score for each candidate fix: (impact x urgency x confidence) / effort, each 1-5, scored with the user.

## Rules

- Stay neutral: do not assume the employees or the managers are at fault before the evidence says so.
- Do not diagnose individuals' health, personality or personal lives; focus on work, skills and conditions.
- If the user describes harassment, discrimination, safety violations or legal risk, say it needs HR or legal involvement and do not treat it as a performance problem.
